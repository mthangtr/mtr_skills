# Reference teardown

What the reference work actually does, image by image. Open the image before copying a move: the description is a pointer, the picture is the evidence. Sources are public X posts and gallery sites, saved Nov 2026 for study only. Never reproduce one of them; take the mechanism.

## The one pattern under all of them

**World first, UI second.** Each piece starts from one picture of a *world* (a sky, a forest, a dune, a marble statue, a golden chess piece, a field of ASCII), treats that picture so it looks designed rather than downloaded, takes its palette from it, and then puts a very quiet UI on top. The UI is almost boring: white or near-black chrome, one small button, a thin nav. The world carries the brand.

## Anti-reference: the four default AI looks (from @tobiadonadon_)

1. **Paper edition**: serif headline with one italic word in the accent colour, mono labels everywhere, "now booking", a city pair in the corner.
2. **Launch page**: black, purple glow, tilted screenshot.
3. **Dashboard**: four KPI cards with sparklines.
4. **Dark dev tool.**
And the overcorrection, **studio portfolio**: off-white, giant headline, numbered mono labels, a clock in the header. If a page shares three features with any of these, go back to the concept.

**Contradictions this round corrected in SKILL.md:**
- Display weight is not always light. 500 is the most common weight, and 600–700 tight type appears in strong work.
- Centred headlines are fine over a product frame.
- Pill CTAs are as common as rectangles.
- Mono suits dev and infra tools as an instrument voice.
- Documentary photos stay untreated.
- The two-tone headline is now a known AI tell when it repeats.
